Simon Fraser University · 2016

Immersion in mobile VR vs. a traditional HMD

Experimental design

I conducted a study with 30 healthy adult participants to compare the immersive experience between mobile VR and a traditional head-mounted display. We used Cardboard VR with a smartphone for the mobile VR group, and the Oculus Rift DK2 as the traditional HMD. Participants played Cryoblast [1], a VR game for pain management, on Cardboard, Oculus Rift and Desktop (control). Jennett's Immersive Experience Questionnaire (IEQ) [2] was used to collect responses after playing. The study was a between-subjects comparison of immersion across the three platforms.

Results

Immersion across the different displays was analysed using a one-way between-subjects ANOVA. The analysis revealed a significant effect of immersion for the three displays at p<0.05 [F(2, 27) = 8.7824, p = 0.0012].

Post hoc analysis using the Tukey-Kramer HSD indicated the mean scores for the Oculus Rift (M = 115.5, SD = 18.08) and the Cardboard VR (M = 109, SD = 21.48) were significantly different from the Desktop display (M = 85.6, SD = 7.51). However, the Cardboard VR did not significantly differ from the Oculus Rift (p = 0.6658) in this study — meaning Cardboard VR, despite having the smallest display screen, was capable of providing a significant immersive experience compared to the high-end Oculus Rift DK2.

Jennett's IEQ measures five underlying factors of immersion — challenge, control, real-world dissociation, emotional involvement and cognitive involvement. We analysed the data further to understand the effects of these five factors for the Oculus Rift and Cardboard VR. With one independent variable (display type) and five dependent variables, a one-way MANOVA was performed in SPSS. The analysis revealed significant effects of the five factors among the three displays, F(5, 23) = 313.604, p<0.05, Wilk's Lambda = 0.014.
